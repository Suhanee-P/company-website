## The brief

WhatIWear started with a founder's idea rather than a specification: an assistant that helps people decide what to wear, built around AI rather than a static catalogue. The founder needed a team that could take that vision, shape it into a product and build it, without a large in-house engineering group.

## What we did

We worked with the founder from the first conversation to define what the assistant should actually do for a user, then designed the flows and built the product. AI style features live or die on whether the suggestions feel personal and sensible, so much of the work went into how the assistant asks questions, how it uses what it learns about the user, and how it explains a recommendation rather than just showing one.

The founder's own words describe the working relationship better than we can: from day one the team understood the vision and executed it, and the collaboration helped WhatIWear get its journey started.

## Why it matters if you are building something similar

Early-stage AI products need three things at once: a clear idea of the user's problem, a design that makes the AI feel helpful rather than gimmicky, and engineering that ships a first version quickly enough to learn from real users. This project is an example of doing all three with a small team.
